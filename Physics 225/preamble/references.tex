\section*{Main References}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Main References}

We shall not have a main textbook for the class. I used Schutz, supplemented by the first edition of d’Inverno,
when I first started learning GR. I think they are both very good texts. However, I have my own way of
presenting GR that will not always follow the structure or presentation of any single book. My approach is
closest in style to the treatments of Liang+Zhou and Wald.
There are numerous sources you can refer to. I highly recommend that you look at different texts to help
understand my lectures better. Here are some good books I’ve used on numerous occasions.

\subsubsubsection*{For General Relativity}
\begin{enumerate}
	\item R d’Inverno, J Vickers, Introducing Einstein’s Relativity: A Deeper Understanding, 2022
	\item C Liang and B Zhou, Differential Geometry and General Relativity, 2023
	\item R Wald, General Relativity, 1984
	\item V Ferrari, L Gualtieri, P Pani, General Relativity and its Applications, 2021
	\item T Padmanabhan, Gravitation: Foundations and Frontiers, 2010
	\item A Zee, Einstein Gravity in a Nutshell, 2013.
	\item S Carroll, Spacetime and Geometry: An Introduction to General Relativity, 2003
	\item Choquet-Bruhat, Introduction to General Relativity, Black Holes, and Cosmology, 2015
	\item J Hartle, Gravity: An Introduction to Einstein’s General Relativity, 2003. (undergrad)
	\item B Schutz, A First Course in General Relativity 2e, 2009. (undergrad)
	\item MP Hobson, GP Efstathiou, and AN Lasenby, General Relativity: An Introduction for Physicists, 2006.
(strong for cosmology)
\end{enumerate}
