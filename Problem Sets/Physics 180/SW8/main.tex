	\hrule\vspace{5pt}
		Your non-physics major friend saw on YouTube that matter and 
		antimatter could annihilate to produce photons. This friend then comes 
		to you claiming to have discovered something profound: “The inverse 
		process of creating matter plus antimatter from a single photon such as
		\begin{equation}
			\label{eq:gammatoe}
		    \gamma \to e^+ + e^-
		\end{equation}
		must occur!” Convince your friend who only learns physics from 
		YouTube (instead of learning from yourself\textsuperscript{\texttrademark}) that this process can not 
		occur
		\vspace{5pt}
	\hrule
	\begin{proof}
		For a photon, it has four momentum
		\begin{equation}
			\label{eq:photon}
			p_\gamma^\mu p_{\gamma \mu} = p_\gamma^2 = 0
		\end{equation}
		Four momentum conservation requires the positron and electron momenta to equal the photon
		\[
			p_\gamma = p_- + p_+
		\]
		Hence
		\[
			0 = p_\gamma^2 = (p_- + p_+)^2
		\]
		But, since $p_-^2 = p_+^2 = m_e^2 c^2 > 0$, then
		\begin{align*}
			(p_- +p_+)^2 &= p_-^2 + p_+^2 + 2p_-\cdot p_+ \\
			             &= 2m_e^2 c^2 + 2p_- \cdot p_+\\
						 &>0
		\end{align*}
		A contradiction. Therefore
		\begin{equation}
			\label{eq:result}
		    \gamma \not\to e^- + e^+
		\end{equation}
		Pair production requires another particle such as a second photon,
		a proton, or a nucleus to conserve four-momentum
	\end{proof}
